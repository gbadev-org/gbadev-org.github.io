The event was hosted by the the gbadev.netopen in new window community. We're promoting the development of new GBA homebrew games, development tools and documentation. Join us on Discord or IRC and check out our curated collection of gbadev resources.

This year we ran another GBA Jam, which had an incredible turnout! There were so many high quality entries (56 in total), with several ties among them. It was real tough to pick out the winners.

A huge thank you and congratulations to everyone who took part, as well as everyone who donatedopen in new window to the prize pool, our fellow organisers and judges. Finally, thanks to our generous sponsors, whose support made this event possible:

incube8 Gamesopen in new window, insideGadgetsopen in new window, Epilogueopen in new window, Bitmap Bureauopen in new window and Game Bubopen in new window.

If you've played and enjoyed the entries so far, then a special thanks to you too. If you haven't then... you know what to do. :)

# Judging and Prizes

The GBA Jam 2026 submissions were judged by: PyroPyro, GValiente, kva64, exelotl, colonelsalt, lifning, evanbowman, skylyrac, Afska.

If you won a prize, we'll be in touch via email over the next few days! More details on how the prizes were calculated can be found hereopen in new window.

As per our policy on multiple entries from a single team, Damage Report and Dungeon Tactics (both made by Tapir Labs) claimed both 1st and 3rd place, they will keep their score placement but will only be eligible to claim prizes for their highest ranking game, Damage Report. The prize structure for 3rd place and below will shift down 1 level.

# Tie-breaks

Tie-breaks have been applied only where necessary for physical prizes to be assigned. Consequently we had to resolve a 2-way tie for 1st place between Damage Report and It matters, a 2-way tie for 3rd place between Dungeon Tactics and The Artifact, and 4-way tie for 6th place between MORDOBOY GBA, Starfall Advance, Witch×Bullet, and Pinball Galaxy Demo Ver.

To break the ties, we ran 8 polls in parallel, asking judges to vote on which of each pair of games they liked the most. This gave the following results:
